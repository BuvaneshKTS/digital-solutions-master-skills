# Visual Craft and Interface Composition

This guide helps move a screen from functional to visually intentional.

## Composition before decoration
Start with the viewport and the content's natural priorities. Establish a grid, key alignment edges, focal point, and scan path. Use proportion and whitespace to separate major regions. Vary section composition where the content has different roles; do not make every section identical.

## Visual weight
Visual weight is affected by size, contrast, color saturation, density, position, typography, and imagery. Make the most important action or information visually dominant. Reduce competing emphasis. Avoid making every card, badge, and heading shout.

## Spacing and alignment
Use a consistent spacing scale. Align related labels, values, controls, and card edges. Check internal padding and gaps at both component and page level. Do not use arbitrary spacing to patch a weak layout.

## Typography
Define a clear type hierarchy for page title, section heading, body, supporting text, labels, and numeric values. Use font weight and size intentionally. Avoid excessive all-caps labels, too many weights, cramped line-height, and tiny secondary text.

## Surfaces and depth
Choose a deliberate surface system: flat, bordered, tonal, or elevated. Depth should clarify layering and interaction. Avoid stacking shadows, borders, gradients, and translucent effects without a purpose.

## Color
Assign semantic roles to colors. Keep primary actions and important states distinct. Use accent colors sparingly enough that they retain meaning. Check text and control contrast.

## Image and illustration
Use visuals to explain, motivate, or establish identity. Choose a consistent illustration/photo style and intentional crop. Do not add decorative images merely to fill empty space. Preserve supplied brand assets and important content.

## Final visual inspection
Inspect the whole screen at intended size, then inspect dense details. Look for uneven rhythm, inconsistent alignment, cramped text, accidental wrapping, competing focal points, clipped content, and decorative noise. Check at least one narrow viewport when responsive behavior matters.
