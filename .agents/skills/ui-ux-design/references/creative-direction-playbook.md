# Creative Direction Playbook

Design with a product-specific visual idea rather than applying a generic template.

## Start with product character
Write a thesis: "[Product] should feel [qualities] because [audience and context]." Tie each quality to the user's task.

## Explore distinct directions
Explore at least three materially different concepts for substantial design tasks. Vary composition, typography, palette, surface treatment, shape language, and imagery—not merely accent colors. Possible directions include editorial, utilitarian, expressive, premium, warm, and data-dense. Choose based on product fit, not trendiness.

## Compose with intent
- Establish one primary focal area and a clear scan path.
- Use hierarchy, scale, alignment, contrast, and whitespace before decoration.
- Avoid monotonous stacks of identical cards.
- Let important content have room; keep dense information scannable.
- Use asymmetry only when it improves emphasis or narrative.
- Avoid gradients, glows, glassmorphism, blobs, and shadows by default.

## Typography and color
Define semantic roles before choosing values. Use a restrained type scale and weight range. Color should communicate brand, action, surface, and status—not decorate every component. Check contrast and color-blind legibility.

## Reference research
Choose references for a specific quality, extract the principle, and reinterpret it. Do not reproduce distinctive layouts, assets, or brand styling.

## Reject these anti-patterns
- Generic SaaS styling applied to every product.
- Cosmetic reskins that retain weak hierarchy.
- Excessive pills, rounded cards, badges, gradients, and shadows.
- Tiny low-contrast metadata.
- Decorative icons without semantic purpose.
- Unrequested features or metrics added to make a screen look fuller.

## Originality check
Ask whether the design clearly belongs to this product, whether its visual concept can be described in one sentence, and whether removing decorative effects would make it clearer.
